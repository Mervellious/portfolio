My story

Before moving into UX/UI, I spent ten years working across film, fashion and advertising, sourcing, styling and designing for different creative projects. It was a world I loved, and one that taught me to think visually, solve problems and bring ideas to life.

But there was something I kept coming back to. So much of what I created was temporary, often centred around entertainment and consumption. Once a shoot was over, sets were dismantled, materials were cleared away and the work became a memory.

I started thinking about what it would mean to create something more lasting, something people return to, use as part of their everyday lives, and that ultimately makes their lives a little easier. That curiosity eventually led me to UX/UI design.

Curious first.
Designer second.

I've always been fascinated by what drives people's decisions, why we choose one thing over another, what influences our behaviour and what's going on in our minds when we make those choices.

As a kid, I used to make random little surveys for family and friends, just for fun. Looking back, it was the researcher in me.

How I work with AI

I use different prompting techniques depending on the project. The ones I use most are the CO-STAR framework, the CREATE framework, RTF (Role, Task, Format) and meta prompting. Alongside these, this is my essential prompt checklist:

My prompt checklist:

  1. 01Stack and versions (e.g. PHP 8, Bootstrap 5)
  2. 02Constraints: e.g. mobile-first, accessible, no inline styles
  3. 03Output format: files, comments, explanation
  4. 04Security requirements: prepared statements, escaped output
  5. 05Ask it to explain its choices
  6. 06Ask for a plan first, and approve it before any code is written
  7. 07Give it a visual reference, not just words

What I always do myself:

  1. 01Check the result at different screen sizes before I accept it
  2. 02Write my own copy, using AI only to tidy grammar
